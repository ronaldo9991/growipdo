## Three biggest gaps

1. No public account of the coordinated regulatory actions
   Sarwa's public growth narrative does not appear to address the DFSA and FSRA enforcement actions over an offer of parent-company shares without an approved prospectus. It also does not publicly explain the DFSA finding that financial information was withheld and positive metrics gave potential investors a misleading impression of the financial performance and position of the DIFC and ADGM entities, or the independent governance review required by the FSRA. Investors, regulators and enterprise clients will treat this as a material disclosure and governance issue, especially because the two regulators coordinated their investigations. Silence leaves unanswered whether the underlying control failures were identified, remediated and independently tested.
   Fix: Publish a factual statement covering the findings, accountability, remediation completed, governance-review outcome and ongoing controls. Evidence: F1, F3, F8, F9, F10, F37, F38, F45.

2. Headline business metrics lack independent support
   The public narrative makes large claims about client assets, profitability, users and funding, but the findings identify these as company-stated rather than independently confirmed. This includes the client-assets milestone, sustained profitability, registered-user count, funding rounds and the claim that a major investor led the Series B round. A serious investor or journalist will distinguish audited, regulator-filed or independently reported performance data from company announcements. Without a clear basis, scope and date for each metric, the claims are difficult to diligence and may invite comparison with the regulator's finding about potentially misleading positive financial metrics.
   Fix: State the calculation date, entity scope and independent assurance or source for each headline metric, and stop presenting unverified figures as settled fact. Evidence: F17, F20, F21, F23, F25, F34, F36, F37, F63, F70.

3. Regulatory structure and product boundaries are not clearly explained
   The record shows a withdrawn DIFC licence alongside an active ADGM firm with specified permissions, yet the public narrative does not clearly explain the change in operating structure, which legal entity provides each product, or the current permissions that apply. Product claims such as trading, crypto access, estimated returns, halal options and tail-risk hedging are company-stated, while the company also states it lacks an Islamic Window endorsement. Clients and enterprise counterparties need to know which regulated entity is responsible for their assets, advice and transactions, and whether product marketing matches permissions and disclosures. Ambiguity is especially consequential for a retail financial-services business with a recent prospectus-related enforcement record.
   Fix: Create a plain-English regulatory and product map naming the responsible entity, active permissions, client protections and key product limitations. Evidence: F5, F7, F16, F29, F50, F51, F61, F64, F65, F66, F67, F69.
