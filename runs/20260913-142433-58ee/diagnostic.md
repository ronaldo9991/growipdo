## Three biggest gaps

1. No public account of the regulatory actions
   Chahwan's public-facing story needs a direct account of the coordinated FSRA and DFSA actions against Sarwa. The regulators found offers of securities were made without an approved prospectus, the DFSA found omitted financial information and positive metrics that could mislead potential investors, and the FSRA required an independent governance review. Investors, regulators, and enterprise partners will find these primary-source records quickly. Silence leaves unanswered questions about leadership accountability, remediation, governance changes, and whether affected investor communications were corrected.
   Fix: Publish a factual statement from Chahwan covering the findings, accountability, completed and ongoing remediation, independent governance review, and controls now governing investor communications. Evidence: F1, F2, F3, F4, F6, F40, F44, F52, F53, F54, F55.

2. Headline business metrics lack independent support
   Sarwa's public narrative cites profitability, client assets under management, user scale, funding, and growth, but the supplied record identifies these as company statements rather than independently confirmed figures. This includes the claim of a client-assets milestone, profitability, registered-user count, funding rounds, and past growth. For a regulated wealth business, serious counterparties need to distinguish audited, regulated, or independently validated operating measures from marketing claims. The lack of third-party support is especially material given the DFSA finding concerning financial information and positive metrics in the public offer.
   Fix: State which operating and financial metrics are independently assured or regulator-filed, give their measurement dates and definitions, and avoid presenting unverified claims as established facts. Evidence: F16, F23, F24, F26, F28, F37, F39, F40, F70.

3. The corporate and regulatory story is not clearly reconciled
   The public record shows a DIFC company whose DFSA registration was withdrawn, alongside an active ADGM firm with separate permissions, yet the public narrative does not plainly explain the entity structure, transfer of regulated activity, or practical client implications. It also contains differing establishment accounts, including a date of establishment and a statement that the business started in a different year. Clients and enterprise partners need clarity on which legal entity serves them, which regulator supervises each activity, and what changed after the DFSA withdrawal. Inconsistent company-history claims weaken confidence in basic corporate disclosure.
   Fix: Create a clear entity-and-regulation page that identifies each Sarwa legal entity, its current permissions, the reason and effect of the DFSA withdrawal, and one sourced company timeline. Evidence: F7, F13, F15, F17, F20, F30, F45, F57, F58, F62, F63, F64.
