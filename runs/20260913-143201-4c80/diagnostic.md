## Summary

Mark Chahwan appears publicly as the Group CEO and co-founder of Sarwa, but the strongest verified record is shaped by regulatory action rather than company messaging. The DFSA fined Sarwa Digital Wealth Limited USD 191,100 and the FSRA fined Sarwa Digital Wealth (Capital) Limited USD 122,500, both for offers of securities without an approved prospectus. The DFSA also said Sarwa withheld financial information and gave positive metrics that could mislead investors, while the FSRA required an independent review of governance. Sarwa reversed committed subscriptions after being told of the concerns, and the two regulators said they worked closely on the case. Several claims about Sarwa’s scale, funding, profitability, user base, and founder story are only the company’s own word and were not independently confirmed. The public picture is therefore of a founder-led fintech with regulated status, but one that has faced serious compliance findings in both DIFC and ADGM.

## Three biggest gaps

1. No public account of the coordinated regulatory action
   Chahwan's public positioning does not appear to address the coordinated DFSA and FSRA enforcement actions against Sarwa. The regulators found unapproved public offers of securities, and the DFSA said Sarwa withheld financial information while presenting positive metrics that could mislead potential investors about the financial position and performance of the DIFC and ADGM entities. The record also includes financial penalties and an FSRA requirement for an independent review of governance arrangements. For investors, regulators, enterprise clients, and journalists, this is the central trust and governance issue in the public record. Silence leaves unanswered what failed, who was accountable, what changed, and whether corrective actions were completed.
   Fix: Publish a factual leadership statement explaining the breaches, remediation, governance-review outcome, controls now in place, and how compliance oversight is reported to the board. Evidence: F1, F3, F7, F8, F9, F40, F41, F46, F49.

2. Headline business metrics lack independent support
   Public claims about profitability, client assets under management, registered users, fundraising, and the funding-round lead are supported in this record mainly by Sarwa's own statements or republication of those statements. The profitability claim also differs between sources on when profitability began and when full-year profitability was reached. A serious counterparty will distinguish audited or independently substantiated performance from company-reported marketing figures. This matters especially because regulators previously found that positive metrics presented to potential investors could create a misleading impression.
   Fix: State the reporting date, metric definition, entity scope, and independent assurance or audit basis for each major financial, funding, and customer metric. Evidence: F16, F22, F23, F25, F26, F27, F37, F40.

3. Corporate and regulatory structure is not clearly reconciled
   The public record spans a withdrawn DIFC-regulated entity and an active ADGM entity, while Sarwa's public materials make broad claims that the company or products are fully licensed and regulated. The DIFC firm's regulatory status ended on 14 June 2024, whereas the ADGM firm remains active and is approved for specified regulated activities; the public narrative should plainly distinguish the entities, jurisdictions, services, and current permissions. There are also differing establishment accounts, with sources describing 2017 and 2018. Clients and regulators need to know exactly which legal entity provides each service, holds client relationships, and is subject to which regulator. Ambiguity can create confusion about protections, accountability, and the accuracy of marketing claims.
   Fix: Maintain a prominently linked entity-and-licensing page that maps every Sarwa product to its legal entity, regulator, permissions, client protections, and current status, with one reconciled company timeline. Evidence: F4, F13, F15, F17, F19, F20, F21, F29, F31, F57, F58, F66.
