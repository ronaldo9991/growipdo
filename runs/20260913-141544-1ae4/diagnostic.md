## Three biggest gaps

1. No clear public accountability for the regulatory actions
   The public record supplied does not show a clear statement from Mark Chahwan addressing the coordinated DFSA and FSRA findings, the penalties, or what changed in response. Regulators found an unapproved public securities offer, misleadingly positive financial information, and required an independent review of Sarwa's governance arrangements. Investors, regulators and enterprise partners will want evidence that senior leadership understands the failures and can show remediation, rather than treating the matters as entity-level legal events. The actions involved both UAE financial regulators and covered communications to almost 100,000 users.
   Fix: Publish a specific leadership statement covering the findings, governance-review status, completed controls, accountable owners and ongoing reporting. Evidence: F1, F7, F29, F30, F36, F39.

2. Headline growth and funding claims lack independent support
   Key claims about client assets, registered users, funding and the Series B round are supported in the supplied record mainly by Sarwa's own materials or announcements repeating company statements. The profitability claim is reported, but the record does not provide independently audited financial detail or independent confirmation for the larger operating and funding headlines. A serious investor or commercial counterparty will distinguish between company-reported marketing metrics and externally validated performance. Without definitions, dates, audit status and third-party support, it is difficult to assess scale, unit economics and capital position.
   Fix: Issue a dated metrics and financial-disclosure pack that defines client assets and users, identifies the reporting basis, and provides independent support where available. Evidence: F13, F17, F18, F19, F20, F27, F49, F56.

3. The public explanation of entities, permissions and product terms is unclear
   Sarwa's public-facing regulatory descriptions are largely company-stated, while the official record shows a DIFC entity withdrew on 14 June 2024 and an ADGM entity remains active. The supplied materials also contain an unresolved inconsistency around Sarwa Trade being described as zero-commission while another company description states charges for stocks and ETFs. Clients and regulators need to know which legal entity provides each service, what permissions apply, where client assets are held, and what customers actually pay. Ambiguity is especially sensitive for an investing and trading platform following regulatory action concerning public-offer communications.
   Fix: Publish a regulator-linked entity and product map with current permissions, client-asset arrangements, applicable jurisdictions and a single authoritative fee schedule. Evidence: F5, F24, F26, F42, F46, F50.
