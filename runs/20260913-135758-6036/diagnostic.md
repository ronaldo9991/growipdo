## Three biggest gaps

1. No public accounting for regulatory enforcement
   There is no visible founder-level explanation of the regulatory actions against Sarwa, the conduct that led to them, or the corrective measures now in place. The FSRA found an offer of securities without an approved prospectus and required an independent review of Sarwa's governance arrangements; the DFSA also found omitted financial information and selectively positive metrics that could mislead investors. Investors, regulators and enterprise clients will treat governance and disclosure controls as a central diligence issue. Silence leaves them to rely on the regulators' account rather than management's explanation and remediation plan.
   Fix: Publish a clear statement from Chahwan covering the findings, accountability, governance-review progress and the controls introduced to prevent recurrence. Evidence: F1, F3, F18, F19, F21, F34.

2. Unclear regulatory structure after the DIFC withdrawal
   Sarwa's public regulatory story needs a current, plain-English explanation of which legal entity serves which customers and activities following the withdrawal of Sarwa Digital Wealth Limited's DFSA licence. The ADGM entity remains active, while the former DIFC entity's register entry records a withdrawal date, yet several licence and regulatory descriptions rely only on Sarwa's own statements. A serious counterparty needs to know the precise regulated entity, permissions and client protections that apply before engaging. Ambiguity is especially material for a financial-services company that has recently faced enforcement over securities offers.
   Fix: Maintain an entity-by-entity public regulatory map that states current permissions, jurisdictions, customer coverage and the implications of the DIFC withdrawal. Evidence: F8, F13, F22, F24, F31, F32.

3. Limited independently supported proof behind headline scale
   The public record supports that Sarwa surpassed $1 billion in client assets under management, but the figure appears in a regulator announcement that also cites Sarwa and in Sarwa's own communications, rather than through independent reporting, audited disclosure or a transparent methodology. There is also no public explanation from Chahwan of how client assets are measured, what portion is discretionary, or how the figure relates to revenue, retention or business durability. Asset figures can signal scale, but investors and enterprise clients need definitions and independently checkable operating evidence to assess the quality of that scale. This is more important given the DFSA finding concerning financial information and positive metrics presented to potential investors.
   Fix: Pair headline asset claims with a dated methodology, key operating definitions and independently supportable performance disclosures. Evidence: F34, F40.
