## Three biggest gaps

1. No clear public account of regulatory breaches and remediation
   The public record includes enforcement action by both the DFSA and the FSRA for offering securities without an approved prospectus. It also includes financial penalties and an FSRA requirement for an independent review of Sarwa's governance arrangements, but the findings provide no public account from Mark Chahwan explaining what failed, who was accountable, what changed, and whether the review's recommendations were completed. Investors, regulators, and enterprise partners will treat repeated prospectus-related enforcement across jurisdictions as a governance and controls question. Silence leaves them to infer that remediation may be incomplete or undisclosed.
   Fix: Publish a factual statement from Mark Chahwan covering the breaches, remediation steps, governance-review outcome, and the controls now used for any future securities communications. Evidence: F1, F2, F3, F14, F15, F16, F17.

2. Unclear legal-entity and regulatory-status narrative
   Sarwa's DIFC entity has a recorded withdrawal date, while the ADGM entity is active and has approved activities for managing assets and arranging deals in investments. The public-facing claims use broad language such as fully licensed and regulated, but the findings do not show a plain explanation of which Sarwa entity provides each product, which regulator oversees it, and what the DIFC withdrawal means for customers and counterparties. A regulated financial-services company needs precise entity-level disclosures, especially where products, client assets, and marketing may cross legal entities. Ambiguity can create diligence concerns about permissions, customer protections, and contractual counterparties.
   Fix: Provide a current entity-and-product map that names the contracting entity, regulator, permissions, and client protections for each Sarwa service. Evidence: F9, F10, F11, F22, F23, F26, F32, F33, F34, F36.

3. Too many company-stated headline claims without independent support
   Several prominent business claims, including funding, registered users, licensing descriptions, and product features, are supported only by Sarwa's own communications rather than independent or regulatory confirmation. One trading-price claim also conflicts with Sarwa's later statement that stock and ETF trades carry a commission, while claims of inclusion in a Forbes Fintech list were not verified. Journalists and investors will discount headline metrics and accolades when sourcing is weak or claims conflict. This can reduce confidence in the accuracy of broader growth, product, and market-positioning statements.
   Fix: Use dated, source-linked disclosures for material metrics and awards, and correct or retire public claims that cannot be independently substantiated. Evidence: F7, F8, F37, F38, F40, F42, F43, F47, F48.
